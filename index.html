Learn Information Design with Phoebe

An infographic is an explanation first: every mark on the page either carries the mechanism or it goes. Six sessions on the one-page explainer of how something works: telling explanation from decoration, guiding the eye along a reading path, drawing the mechanism itself, picking the right explaining form, and putting labels where the parts are. One constructed running case, a water utility explaining what happens between the reservoir and your tap, and one bench that measures a page's path, labels, contrast and overlaps as you move it.

The six sessions 🧭 · from a decorated draft to a measured explainer

The running case is Wrenmoor Water, a constructed town water utility. After a boil-water notice, customers kept asking what happens to the water between the reservoir and their tap. Nia, who runs communications, owes them a one-page explainer; Owen, an engineer at the treatment works, knows every step. Her first draft is about water. Over six sessions it becomes an explanation of how water reaches you. Wrenmoor and everyone in it are invented for teaching, and every page says so.

🧭 Session 1 · foundations

Decoration or explanation

The one-pager's job is to explain how something works. The chartjunk debate read at source, three studies that measured three different things, and the four-way test every mark has to pass.

▶ Start here 🧭 Session 2 · core

The reading path

Where the eye enters, how numbers and arrows carry it from step to step, and why each step's words belong beside its picture. The F-pattern read for what it actually says.

entry, sequence, contiguity ⚙️ Session 3 · core

Draw the mechanism

Let the subject carry the page: cutaway, exploded view and process drawing. Isotype's transformer, the person between the expert and the artist, and what to leave out.

the subject carries the page 🗂️ Session 4 · deeper

Five explaining forms

Process, anatomy, comparison, map and timeline: the question each one answers, how to pick one for a brief, and why charts belong to a different craft.

pick the form by the question 🏷️ Session 5 · deeper

Labels, leader lines and callouts

Names beside the parts they name, leader lines that never cross a word, the eight candidate positions cartographers use, callouts as signals, and contrast that clears WCAG 2.2.

split attention, fixed 📐 Session 6 · bench night

The explainer bench

A constructed one-pager measured in your browser: path length and crossings, label distance and lookups, every contrast ratio and every overlap. Four fixes, a tidy-looking trap and a break button.

What is honest here 🧾

Design advice travels with famous research, and the decoration debate is quoted on both sides. Each claim on these pages carries its status.

ClaimStatus
